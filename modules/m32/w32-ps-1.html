The Settlement and the Dictators
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

On January 8, 1918, President Woodrow Wilson spoke to Congress and set out the terms on which he thought the war should end. Germany later asked for an armistice hoping for peace on these terms. As you read, ask what Wilson hopes the world will look like, what he asks of the great powers, and which points would be hardest to carry out.

I. Open covenants of peace, openly arrived at, after which there shall be no private international understandings of any kind, but diplomacy shall proceed always frankly and in the public view.

II. Absolute freedom of navigation upon the seas, outside territorial waters, alike in peace and in war.

V. A free, open-minded, and absolutely impartial adjustment of all colonial claims, in which the interests of the populations concerned must have equal weight with the claims of the governments.

VIII. All French territory should be freed and the invaded portions restored, and the wrong done to France in 1871 in the matter of Alsace-Lorraine should be righted.

XIV. A general association of nations must be formed under specific covenants for the purpose of affording mutual guarantees of political independence and territorial integrity to great and small states alike.
