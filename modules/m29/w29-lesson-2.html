🎯 TODAY'S FOCUS

In 1850 there was no Italy and no Germany, only a patchwork of kingdoms, duchies, and cities. By 1871 both were nations. Today you will meet three men who helped to bring this about, and ask how much of it was their doing and how much would have happened anyway.

ITALY: A GEOGRAPHIC EXPRESSION

After 1815 Metternich called Italy a mere geographic expression. Austria held Lombardy and Venetia in the north, the pope ruled Rome and the center, a Bourbon king held the south, and the little kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia, ruled by the House of Savoy, sat in the northwest. The movement to unite them was called the risorgimentoree-sor-jee-MEN-tohAn Italian word meaning "resurgence" or "rising again." It names the movement to unite Italy in the 1800s. See the glossary box.. It had poets, conspirators, and dreamers. Giuseppe Mazzini, an exile and a passionate republican, wanted an Italian republic and founded the group Young Italy. His risings failed again and again, and in 1849 a short-lived republic in Rome was crushed by French troops.

The man who finally succeeded was a practical statesman. Count Camillo di Cavour became prime minister of Piedmont in 1852. He was not a soldier or a romantic. He was a landowner who built railways, cut tariffs, and strengthened the army. He knew that Piedmont alone could not defeat Austria, so he looked for an ally. He sent troops to join Britain and France in the Crimean War, which won him a place at the peace table. In 1858 he made a secret agreement with Napoleon III of France: in return for Nice and Savoy, France would help to drive Austria from northern Italy.

WAR, VOLUNTEERS, AND A KINGDOM

War came in 1859. The French and Piedmontese won the bloody battles of Magenta and Solferino. But Napoleon III grew alarmed at the cost and at the nationalist forces he had set loose. Without warning he signed a peace with Austria, leaving Venetia in Austrian hands. Cavour was furious and resigned. Even so, Piedmont received Lombardy, and the people of Tuscany and other central states voted to join it in 1860.

Then came the second figure. Giuseppe Garibaldi was a seaman and a famous guerrilla fighter. In May 1860 he sailed from Genoa with about a thousand volunteers in red shirts and landed in Sicily. Against all odds, they defeated the Bourbon troops, crossed to the mainland, and marched into Naples. Cavour did not trust Garibaldi, who was a republican, but he also could not stop him. Garibaldi, to his credit, put the good of Italy above his own ambitions. He met King Victor Emmanuel II of Piedmont and handed him the whole south. In March 1861 the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed. Cavour died that June, only a few months later. The new kingdom was not an easy one to govern. The north was prosperous and the south poor, and many southerners felt ruled from far away. The pope refused to recognize the new state for decades. Italy added Venice in 1866 as an ally of Prussia, and Rome in 1870, when the French garrison went home to fight Germany.

PRUSSIA AND ITS MINISTER

Germany's road was different. The king, close to giving up the throne, called on Otto von Bismarck.

Bismarck came from the Junkers, the landed nobility of eastern Prussia. He was a clever diplomat and a believer in realpolitikray-AHL-poh-lee-teekA German word for politics based on power and practical results rather than on ideals. He told the parliament that the great questions would be settled not by speeches and majority votes but by iron and blood, and he ruled for years without a legal budget. Notice what kind of leader he was. He was no romantic nationalist. He wanted a strong Prussia, and he used German nationalism as a tool when it served that purpose.

THREE WARS

His first war came in 1864. Prussia and Austria together defeated Denmark and took the duchies of Schleswig and Holstein. Bismarck then quarreled with Austria over how to govern them, and prepared for war with his partner. He made sure Russia stayed neutral and gained the friendship of Italy. In 1866 the Prussian army, moved by railroad and armed with the breech-loading needle gun, crushed the Austrians at Königgrätz in a matter of weeks. Its chief of staff, Helmuth von Moltke, planned the campaign.

Here Bismarck showed his judgment. The king and the generals wanted to march on Vienna and take land. Bismarck argued against it. He took no Austrian territory, because he wanted Austria as a friend in the future. Austria was excluded from Germany, and the German states north of the Main River joined a North German Confederation under Prussian leadership.

The last step was France. In 1870 a quarrel arose over a Hohenzollern prince's candidacy for the Spanish throne. Bismarck received a telegram from the king's vacation spot at Ems describing a polite meeting with the French ambassador, and he edited it to sound sharper. French opinion was inflamed, and Napoleon III declared war in July. The southern German states joined Prussia, and the German armies won victory after victory. On September 1, 1870, Napoleon III was captured at Sedan. Paris held out through a long siege. On January 18, 1871, in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, King William was proclaimed German Emperor. Bismarck became the first chancellorCHAN-suh-lerThe chief minister of a government in some countries, such as Germany. See the glossary box. of the new empire. France lost Alsace and part of Lorraine, a loss it would not forget.

Compare the cost with the great wars of the past. The fighting that made Germany lasted only a few weeks in 1866 and about ten months in 1870 and 1871. Yet the result was a new great power in the middle of the continent. The new Germany had some forty-one million people, more than France, and its coal and steel output would soon pass Britain's.

WHAT MADE THE NATIONS?

Was it the leaders, or was it deeper forces? In both countries, the answer is a mixture. But the actual shape of the new states depended on particular decisions: Napoleon III's secret bargain, Garibaldi's gamble, King William's choice of Bismarck, and Bismarck's restraint after Königgrätz. A Germany that included Austria was entirely possible. So was an Italy of two or three kingdoms.
