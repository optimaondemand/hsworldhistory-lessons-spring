What this module contains

This Teacher's Module holds the Teacher's Manual for Quarters 3 and 4 (Weeks 19-36). The manual maps every lesson, primary source and graded item to the CPALMS benchmarks of World History, 2109310, and lists plainly which benchmarks the course does not teach. It also covers the Appendix module of supplementary lessons. The course syllabus is not part of this module.

Every code below appears only in teacher-facing materials. Student pages carry no standards codes.

Coverage at a glance

71 benchmarks are in scope for Quarters 3 and 4: the 47 content benchmarks of strands W.4 (voyages and colonies) through W.9, the six W.1 history skills, the six ELA expectations, and the geography, communism and Holocaust benchmarks the course touches.

How the crosswalk was built

The Spring course map names topics and sources but cites no benchmarks, so this mapping is the course builder's, not the map's. It was made from each lesson's objectives and then checked against the lesson text: a benchmark is tagged to a lesson only if that lesson's text treats it. Benchmark wording is transcribed from the CPALMS export in the course's analysis folder.

Taught means students read it in a lesson or primary-source page (L1 and L2 are the week's two lessons, PS its two primary sources). Assessed means a graded item reaches it: the Quarter exam (2 questions per lesson, new facts only), the Semester Final (1 question per lesson), the week's Reading Quiz, Primary Source Report or VR Activity, and the weekly discussion. Questions are not individually tagged to benchmarks. Exam and Final coverage follows the lesson each question is drawn from.

Two statuses need care. Partial means the benchmark is taught but only part of what it names. Taught, not separately assessed applies to W.1.1 and W.1.5, which lessons use throughout (timelines, cause and effect, and the weighing of individual choices against larger forces) but which no graded item is tagged to.

Appendix module: supplementary lessons

The Appendix module holds seven supplementary lessons written to close gaps in the benchmark crosswalk. It sits after Module 36 in Canvas and is not part of the week-by-week schedule. Use it as extension, as make-up for benchmarks your course requires, or as an enrichment path. Each lesson is a standalone reading of 1,000 to 1,300 words. Four reading quizzes (A.8 to A.11, 6 to 7 questions each) follow the lessons, and one 14-question Module Test (A.12) covers all seven, two questions per lesson. All five are auto-graded.

Flagged content decisions
  • Primary-source texts are placeholders. All 36 primary-source pages are plain-text placeholders written from memory and marked as such. The primary-source benchmark tags (W.1.3 and the content tag per source) describe what each source is placed to teach. They hold only once authentic texts replace the placeholders.
  • VR activities assume a reconstruction (Waterloo, a map of Africa, a Western Front trench, a container port). Each teacher note says to substitute and log it if none exists. A substitution changes what the activity reaches.
  • Exams sit at the end of their modules. The Quarter 3 exam is in Module 27 (Weeks 19-27), the Quarter 4 exam in Module 36 (Weeks 28-36), and the Semester Final in Module 36 covers Weeks 19-36 only.
  • The geography benchmarks rest on the student's own map. The plain build carries no maps, so Appendix Lessons A.6 and A.7 ask students to open an atlas or online map while they read. Those five benchmarks (G.2.1, G.2.2, G.2.3, G.4.2, G.4.3) are marked Partial for that reason. Custom maps would close the gap.
  • The mapping rests on the lesson text as written. If a lesson is revised, rerun l1_check_benchmarks.py and regenerate this page.
Standards philosophy, for reference

Every benchmark should be taught by at least one lesson students read and assessed by at least one item students submit. Every graded item should reach at least one benchmark. Benchmarks are spread across lessons rather than built as a one-to-one checklist. Benchmark codes never appear on a student-facing page; they live in this manual.
